      What the city learns

      Each company turns each ride into a Mastic report. On the Pickups tab α is the pickup cell and β has five one-hot blocks: company, pickup hour, car category, surge and drop-off zone. On the Drop-offs tab α is the drop-off cell, the hour is the drop-off hour and the last block is the pickup zone instead. The two aggregators find the cells with at least the threshold number of rides without seeing any single report.

      For each heavy cell the city learns the totals of each block: rides per company, per hour, and so on. The blocks are separate, so it does not learn, for example, Waymo's rides per hour. It learns nothing about individual rides or riders, or about cells below the threshold. The colours come only from those totals.

      Each tab is its own query over the same rides, so with both the city learns the heavy pickup cells and the heavy drop-off cells, each with its block totals. It still does not learn which pickup goes with which drop-off, beyond the three named zones.

      The Neighborhoods tab is a third query with no weights: β is just the count, so the city learns only how many pickups each heavy cell has. The heavy cells are then grouped into neighborhoods afterwards, from those counts alone, which reveals nothing more.

      Companies are assigned synthetically (city/make_company_rides.py); the pickups are real RideAustin data from the busiest day (March 17, 2017).
